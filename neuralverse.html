Walk through a language model: how text is split, placed in space, connected and continued.

Start exploring

Text is cut into tokens

A model never sees letters or whole sentences. It sees tokens: common chunks of text, each mapped to an ID. Type anything and watch it split. Long or rare words break into smaller pieces.

Meaning becomes position

Each token turns into a list of numbers, a point in a huge space. Words with related meanings land close together. Real models use thousands of dimensions; here is a 3D slice. Drag to rotate and click a word to see its neighbours.

Tokens look at each other

Attention lets every token decide which earlier tokens matter for its meaning. Each head learns a different habit. Hover a word to see where it looks, and switch heads to compare.

One word at a time

Finally the model scores every possible next token and picks one. Click a bar to choose it yourself, or let the model sample. Low temperature plays it safe; high temperature takes risks.

Neuralverse is a simplified teaching model. Token splits, embeddings, attention weights and probabilities are illustrative, not taken from a real network.
